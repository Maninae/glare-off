# 01: Existing models we could reuse for client-side eyeglass glare removal

Research date: 2026-10-05. All claims carry a URL. "Could not verify" means I looked and did not find evidence either way. Sizes come from GitHub release / Hugging Face API metadata (no weights were downloaded) unless marked "estimate".

## Takeaway

1. **No eyeglass-specific glare model is publicly usable.** The two serious ones (ER²Net, TCSVT 2024; DL2G, CVPR 2025, both on the ReyeR real-world dataset) have no public code, weights, or dataset that I could find, and the ReyeR face data is stated as "permitted for research purpose only". De-Glared (ICCIT 2023) is a ResearchGate PDF only. take-off-eyeglasses has weights but **no license file** (all rights reserved) and removes the glasses entirely.
2. **General reflection-removal (SIRR) nets transfer well once retrained on eyeglass data:** in DL2G's own benchmark, DSRNet retrained on eyeglass pairs scores 33.6 dB PSNR vs 34.8 dB for the eyeglass-specific SOTA. So the architecture matters less than the training data. Browser-sized, permissively licensed candidates exist: DExNet-S (4.5M params, Apache-2.0), ERRNet (19.3M, MIT), DHAN-SHR specular net (18 MB weights, MIT, but trained on research-only SHIQ data).
3. **Segmentation is solved enough:** `mantasu/glasses-detector` (MIT code) ships a dedicated **lens** segmentation model at 13 MB (LRASPP-MobileNetV3) and ~1 MB (tiny), 256x256. Caveat: it was trained partly on CelebAMask-HQ, which is non-commercial.
4. **Browser inpainting is proven** (MI-GAN, 28-30 MB ONNX, MIT code AND MIT weights; runs in lxfater/inpaint-web). It is fine for glints off the iris but will hallucinate the eye when glare covers the iris, which is exactly the hard case.
5. **removeglassesglare.com is server-side and generative**: its JS calls `api.removeglassesglare.com` with stages IDENTIFICATION → GLARE_REMOVAL → POST_PROCESSING and a "primary"/"secondary" model; its own example reconstructs a pupil that was fully covered by a ring light, which only a generative model can do. The free open-weight equivalent is WindowSeat (Apache-2.0 LoRA on Qwen-Image-Edit-2509), usable offline as a **teacher** to make training pairs, not in the browser.

**Judgment on inpainting vs. restoration.** Inpainting throws away the pixels under the mask and invents new ones. That is acceptable for (a) small saturated glints on the lens away from the iris, and (b) the fully blown-out core of a ring light where no signal survives anyway. It is not acceptable as the main method: most lens glare is a semi-transparent additive veil (blue/green AR-coating tint, window reflections) through which the real iris, gaze direction and eye color are still visible, and a restoration model can recover those while an inpainter replaces them with a generic eye (wrong gaze, wrong color, mismatched left/right). ER²Net's design (elimination for weak reflections, inpainting only for strong ones, fused by per-pixel reflection intensity) is the right shape, and DL2G's numbers back it. Neither MI-GAN nor LaMa was trained to draw eyes behind glass; expect visible artifacts on irises (my inference, not tested).

## Q6. How the commercial tools likely work

Evidence from removeglassesglare.com's own page and JS (fetched 2026-10-05):

- **Server-side, not client-side.** `core.min.js` posts to `https://api.removeglassesglare.com/glare/upload` and fetches `/glare/download-url`; the API is behind Cloudflare (403 on a bare GET, Cloudflare headers), so the backend vendor is not visible. Homepage: "all photos auto-deleted in 24h", 25 MB max, min 400 px, credits from $2, free low-res preview, batch up to 100.
- **Multi-stage pipeline.** `app.min.js` job statuses: `PROCESSING` → `IDENTIFICATION` ("Understanding key areas to treat the image correctly") → `GLARE_REMOVAL` ("Applying a custom AI model") → `POST_PROCESSING` ("Enhancing clarity in specific areas"). This reads as: detect face/lenses/glare, run a model, then paste back / sharpen locally.
- **Two models with routing.** The status response carries `model`; `"secondary"` shows a "Deep analysis" badge, with a progress-creep budget of 100 s vs 30 s for primary, and messages like "Reconstructing certain portions... Recovering fine details hidden behind glare". So a fast primary model plus a slow fallback (plausibly a large diffusion image-editor) for hard cases. This is inference from the UI strings.
- **It is generative.** In their own "ring light" example the reflection fully covers the man's right pupil in the before image, and the after shows a clean, plausible eye. That content cannot be recovered by layer separation; it was synthesized. A pixel-difference map I computed between their before/after shows the big change confined to the lenses, plus small changes across the whole frame including background bokeh lights, which is weakly consistent with a full-image VAE round trip (diffusion editor) but also consistent with re-compression; inconclusive.
- **Industry pattern.** Competitors openly advertise prompt-driven editors: bananaproai.com and media.io pages say they use Nano Banana / Seedream; Fotor asks the user to type a prompt; Adobe's forum thread is about Generative Fill prompts. No public statement from removeglassesglare.com about which model it uses; could not verify. Sources: [bananaproai](https://bananaproai.com/image/ai-remove-glasses-glare/), [media.io](https://www.media.io/ai/image-to-image/remove-glasses-glare-from-photo), [Fotor](https://www.fotor.com/features/remove-glasses-from-photo/), [Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-712/glasses-glare-removal-more-than-just-using-ai-generative-fill-prompts-1162319). No HN/Reddit discussion of the site found.

## Ranked shortlist: what I would actually try first, second, third

**1st: zero-training browser baseline (days, not weeks).** MediaPipe Face Landmarker to crop each eye → glasses-detector `lenses` LRASPP (13 MB, export to ONNX) for the lens mask → in-lens glare mask from luminance/low-saturation thresholds → (a) for the semi-transparent veil, a classical de-veiling step (subtract a smooth estimated reflection layer, recolor toward the unaffected lens region); (b) for saturated cores off the iris, MI-GAN (28 MB, MIT weights). Why first: every piece is permissive-code, already runs in a browser, and it sets the quality bar and shows exactly which cases fail (iris-covering glare). Licensing to-do: the lens model's CelebAMask-HQ training data; retrain it on permissive/synthetic data if we ever go commercial.

**2nd: drop-in small restoration nets on eye crops, no training.** Run DExNet-S (4.5M, Apache-2.0), ERRNet (19.3M, MIT), PINTO's DINOv3 ONNX (89 MB, MIT) and DHAN-SHR (18 MB, MIT code) on the cropped lens regions from step 1 and compare against the baseline on a small set of real glare portraits. Why: zero cost to test, and these see through semi-transparent glare instead of hallucinating. Expectation: partial wins on veils, failures on ring lights, because none saw eyeglass data (DL2G shows retraining is what closes the gap).

**3rd: train our own small eye-crop restorer (the likely real product).** Architecture: DExNet-S or an ERRNet-sized U-Net, 256x256 eye crops, glare-intensity-weighted fusion of a restoration head and an inpainting head (ER²Net's idea). Data, since ReyeR is unavailable and research-only: (a) synthetic pairs by compositing ring-light / window / screen / AR-tint reflection layers onto lens regions of permissively licensed glasses portraits, with physically plausible additive blending and lens-shaped masks; (b) pseudo-pairs from an offline Apache-2.0 teacher (WindowSeat on Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) on real glare photos, filtered by hand. Why third: highest ceiling and the only route to a self-owned, permissively licensed browser model, but it is real ML work. Do 1 and 2 first so we know which failure cases the training data must cover.

Not recommended: take-off-eyeglasses (no license, wrong task), anything built on CelebAMask-HQ, SHIQ, ReyeR or FLUX.1-dev weights if the tool will ever be commercial, and diffusion SIRR models in the browser (GB-scale).
